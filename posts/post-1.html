48 Hours in Tokyo: A First-Timer's Guide

🏯

I arrived at Narita Airport on a Tuesday morning, bleary-eyed after a fourteen-hour flight, armed with a pocket Wi-Fi dongle, a Japan Rail Pass, and absolutely no idea where I was going. What followed was forty-eight of the most exhilarating hours I have ever spent anywhere.

Getting Into the City

The Narita Express (N'EX) whisks you from the airport to Shinjuku Station in about 90 minutes. With the JR Pass it's covered, otherwise budget around ¥3,070 (roughly €19). Avoid the Limousine Bus unless you're carrying heavy luggage – traffic into central Tokyo can be brutal.

Day 1: Shinjuku & Harajuku

Check into a capsule hotel near Shinjuku. They cost around ¥3,500–¥5,000 per night and are a quintessential Tokyo experience. Mine had a tiny locker for my bag, a shared bathhouse, and a little pod that felt weirdly cosy once I got over my mild claustrophobia.

After dropping my bags, I walked the east exit of Shinjuku: golden-lit arcades, department stores the size of city blocks, and street food vendors offering everything from takoyaki to freshly grilled skewers.

"The best ramen I have ever eaten came from an unmarked basement restaurant I found purely by accident, pointed there by a stranger who noticed me staring at a menu I couldn't read."

The next morning I made the pilgrimage to Meiji Shrine in Harajuku: a serene forested path that makes you forget you're inside one of the world's largest cities.

Day 2: Asakusa & Shibuya

Senso-ji Temple in Asakusa opens at sunrise. Get there early and you'll have the main gate – Kaminarimon – almost to yourself. By 9 am it's crowded with tour groups; by noon it's heaving.

Ride the Yamanote Line south to Shibuya. Stand at the famous scramble crossing during rush hour and watch hundreds of people converge from every direction without once colliding. It's choreography on a city scale.

Practical Tips

  • Get a Suica card at the airport – it works on almost every train and bus.
  • 7-Eleven and FamilyMart sell surprisingly good hot meals for under ¥600.
  • Google Maps works excellently for Tokyo transit; download offline maps just in case.
  • Carry cash – many small restaurants and shrines are cash-only.
  • Tipping is not customary in Japan; it can actually cause embarrassment.

Would I Go Back?

Without hesitation. Tokyo rewards repeat visits: each neighbourhood has its own personality and 48 hours barely scratches the surface. Next time I want a full week, a Japan Rail Pass, and a slower itinerary that lets me get truly lost.
