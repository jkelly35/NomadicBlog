Surfing the Silver Coast on a Budget

🌊

Portugal's Algarve gets all the attention, but the real gem for surf travel is the Costa de Prata – the Silver Coast – stretching north of Lisbon to Porto. I spent two weeks hitching between beach towns with a rented longboard and a tent, and I came home with salty hair, sunburnt shoulders, and the most relaxed I'd felt in years.

Getting There

Fly into Lisbon. Budget airlines connect most European cities to Lisbon Humberto Delgado Airport for well under €100 return if you book a few weeks ahead. From the airport, take the Metro to Oriente station and pick up a Rede Expressos coach north.

Peniche – home of the Supertubos break that hosts the WSL Championship Tour – is about 80 minutes from Lisbon by coach and costs around €8. It's the perfect base for exploring the coast in both directions.

The Waves

November is shoulder season: the summer crowds have gone, the Atlantic swell picks up in earnest, and the water temperature is still a manageable 17 °C with a 3/2 mm wetsuit.

  • Supertubos – a heavy beach break, best left to experienced surfers.
  • Baleal – mellow waves, surf schools, ideal for beginners.
  • Consolação – a sheltered bay that holds shape in bigger swells.
  • Areia Branca – a long sandy beach that rarely gets too crowded.

Budget Breakdown

I tracked every euro. Here's my average daily spend over 14 nights:

  • Accommodation (hostel dorm or campsite): €12–18
  • Food (market produce, bakeries, one sit-down meal): €15–20
  • Board hire (6-foot fish, full day): €20
  • Transport (coaches, occasional ride-share): €5

Total average: about €55–63 per day, including the board.

"A grilled sardine at a harbour-side restaurant in Peniche, eaten with crusty bread and a glass of Vinho Verde, cost me €4.50. I ate there four days running."

Wild Camping

Wild camping is technically permitted in Portugal away from protected nature reserves and private land, provided you leave no trace. I camped above the cliffs at Consolação on two nights and woke up to a sunrise over the Atlantic that no hotel breakfast could compete with. Always check local regulations before pitching up.

Getting Around

Rede Expressos and Rodoviária do Oeste coaches connect the main towns, and BlaBlaCar is popular with locals and travellers alike for shorter hops. A bicycle is a genuinely great option for the flat sections between Peniche and São Martinho do Porto.

Final Thoughts

The Silver Coast is one of those places that doesn't punch above its weight – it simply delivers exactly what it promises: beautiful beaches, consistent surf, affordable food, and friendly locals who are quietly pleased you found their stretch of coastline. Go before everyone else does.
